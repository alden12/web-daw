MOBILE-1 · design round 2

The bottom tabs become the desktop's four surfaces

Round one proved the shell can swap. This round fixes what the prototype revealed: tabs that mirrored a phone's idea of an app rather than the desktop's actual workspaces, and a workbench trying to hold two panels on a screen with room for one.

Prototype running on slice-96 Phone 390 × 844 Landscape 844 × 390 Palette: src/index.css

01 — the move

One tab per workspace, not one tab per phone convention

The desktop shows arrangement, editor, clips and devices at once. A phone can show one. So the tab bar stops being navigation invented for mobile and becomes a switch between the four surfaces that already exist — which means the mental model transfers in both directions.

That reframing does something else: it makes the editor the only thing in its tab. The prototype's two worst compromises — the clip rail crammed above the roll, and the device rack fighting the roll for vertical space — were both artefacts of stacking surfaces that now have their own tabs.

The ⋮ menu being context-sensitive is what lets the piano roll's toolbar disappear. Snap, quantize, auto-Q, zoom and note length are roll controls, so they appear in ⋮ only while the roll is open — no second toolbar, and no truncated row of buttons competing for 390 pixels.

03 — the keystone

The selected track's lane never leaves the screen

Above the workspace on every tab except Arrangement: one row, the selected track's own lane, on the same bar grid and playhead as the full arrangement. You can see where the thing you're editing sits in the song, and move it, without switching away.

07 — consequences

What this removes from the prototype

delete

The side-by-side rack

The sideBySide prop, the rack's width state, and the second measurement axis. Devices having a tab means it never competes with the roll for height.

delete

The compact clip rail

compact and the horizontal ClipRail variant. Clips get a tab, so the rail stops being a strip squeezed above the roll.

delete

Tap-to-navigate

onEditTrack on the timeline. Selection stops implying navigation, because the lane strip keeps the selected track on screen anyway.

simplify

The rack height clamp

MIN_EDITOR and the proportional fallback stay as a desktop guard — a real bug either way — but stop carrying the mobile case.

keep

Non-sticky headers

Still right on Arrangement: a pinned 220px column costs the same absolute pixels on a phone as on a desktop.

new

Lane strip + two sheets

One new component and two overlays, against four conditional-layout props removed. The shell gets simpler, not more complex.

08 — decisions

Settled: four agreed, one revised, one elaborated

01

Does Clips earn a tab, or fold into the lane strip?

You flagged it as a maybe. Folding it in would mean tapping the lane strip's track name opens a clip sheet, leaving three tabs and less chrome.

Agreed — keep the tab Mode-less clip launching is one of the things the app has that Ableton's grid doesn't do this way, and it is the single most touch-native surface here: big targets, no precision, immediate sound. A sheet would bury the app's most demo-able feature one tap deeper.
02

Where do mixer controls live now?  · revised

You were right, and my flag was wrong twice over. There is no missing surface and nothing new to build.

It is the Project view, one of the six rail views, so it comes across in the ☰ sheet for free — same component, same name, no "Mixer" invented alongside it. Expanding a track there reveals its mute / solo / gain exactly as on desktop.

And group faders were never missing. I said the bus tree had nowhere to go; in fact every group header in the Arrangement tab already carries mute, solo and a volume fader (GroupHeader), so the whole bus tree is reachable on mobile as it stands. The one real gap is that Project gives detail to tracks but not to groups — true on desktop too, and a separate fix if it bothers you.

Settled — call it Project, build nothing Decision 02 closes with less work than it opened with.
03

Library adds by tap — what does a tap mean?

On desktop you drag an instrument onto a lane. You cannot drag out of an overlay that has to close, so tapping has to decide for you: assign to the selected track, or create a new one?

Agreed — assign if empty, else create Tapping an instrument assigns it to the selected track when that track has none, and otherwise creates a new track in the current group. Effects always append to the selected track's chain. Samples and patches follow the same rule as instruments.
04

Does the lane strip show on the Devices tab?

Devices is the one surface with no relationship to arrangement time, so the strip is arguably 56px of wasted height there.

Agreed — yes, keep it Consistency is worth more than the pixels: the strip is also where you confirm you are editing the right track, and tweaking a filter while a clip loops is exactly when you want the playhead visible.
05

Is the tab label dynamic, or fixed?  · elaborated

Tab 2 is "the editor for the selected track" — but there isn't one editor. Which component you land on already depends on what you selected:

Instrument trackSubtractive, FM, Supersaw, Organ, Mellotron, Wavetable, Nimbus, SamplerPianoRoll
Drum kitIts own pad grid, with the existing Pads / Keys toggleDrumRoll · StepGrid
Audio trackWaveform, loop region, grid slide — no notes at allAudioClipPanel
Empty trackNo instrument chosen yetInstrumentPicker

So the only question is what the tab is called. Two options:

Dynamic — the label follows the selection: "Roll" on a synth, "Pads" on a drum kit, "Sample" on an audio track. You know what you'll get before you tap. The cost is that the tab bar rewrites itself when you change track, so a target you were already reaching for gets renamed mid-reach — which matters more on a phone, where the tab bar is muscle memory.

Fixed — always "Edit". Stable muscle memory, and the icon can still hint at the kind. The cost is it tells you nothing about where you'll land.

Recommendation — dynamic, and make the catalog say so One correction to how I put this the first time: I implied the label could be read off the catalog today. It can't. InstrumentInfo carries type, label, schema, family — no notion of an editor, so CenterWorkbench currently hardcodes kind === "audio" and instrumentType === "drumkit". Doing this properly means adding an editor field to the catalog entry, which pays for itself twice: it drives the tab label and deletes that existing hardcoded branch, so a future instrument declares its editor instead of editing the workbench. That is the version worth building; if you'd rather not touch the catalog in this slice, take the fixed "Edit" instead of a hardcoded label map.
